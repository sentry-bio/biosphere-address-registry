# Normative glossary

**Address** — A map reading registered onto a named Form through a named
transform. It is not a taxonomic label.

**Datum / Form** — The slowly changing reference-frame contract: metric/chart
conventions, dimension, kappa convention, anchors, epsilon, radial proxy, and
conformance gates.

**Evidence** — The operating-point-independent result of reading an
observation with a map: neighborhood, distances, support, typicality, quality,
and rank distributions.

**Interpretation** — A replaceable view over Evidence under a named ontology,
release, and operating point.

**Map edition** — A compatible tokenizer, encoder, reference vectors, index,
metric, routing policy, calibration, and verification bundle.

**Neighborhood** — Ordered references and distances returned under the map's
declared search metric. This is the primary operational reading.

**Observation** — Content-identified sequence material plus normalization,
window, quality, and source provenance.

**Organism record** — An immutable linking record collecting observations,
evidence, addresses, and interpretations. It does not merge their authority.

**Projection** — A disposable 2D/3D rendering basis. Projection coordinates are
not retrieval coordinates or a datum unless separately registered.

**Reference manifest** — A content-addressed finite catalog of reference
genomes/assemblies. Taxonomy is an annotation over the catalog.

**Registry** — An immutable object store plus logged, mutable human refs.

**Replay** — Re-execution of a reading under its original map artifacts and
comparison under declared numerical tolerance.

**Species recording** — A taxonomy-edition collection of genome distributions,
including membership, medoid/barycenter, dispersion, and external neighborhood.
It is not an exact point.

**Transform** — A versioned reduction from one map edition to one Form,
carrying its fit panel, anchors, residuals, inheritance, and certification.

**Window policy** — Deterministic rules for slicing biological material into
map inputs. It is part of measurement semantics.
